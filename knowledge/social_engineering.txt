Social engineering attacks manipulate people psychologically rather
than relying only on technical vulnerabilities.

Common techniques include urgency, fear, authority, rewards,
threats, curiosity, and pressure.

Urgency may appear as phrases suggesting that an account will be
closed or suspended unless immediate action is taken.

Threat-based messages may claim that the recipient will lose access,
face penalties, or experience account restrictions.

Reward-based scams may claim that the recipient has won a prize,
refund, reward, or special offer.

Attackers may impersonate trusted organizations to make requests
appear legitimate.

Users should independently verify suspicious requests through an
official website, application, phone number, or other trusted channel.
